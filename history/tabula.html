David Hartmann · Numeracy Works LLC · Augusta, Georgia

I The story 2008 – 2015

2008 – 2009

Numeracy Works

David Hartmann, who taught geometry and ran the computer lab at Meigs Magnet School in Nashville for ten years, founds Numeracy Works in 2008 with one product prominent in my mind – Tabula: the teaching and learning tool that I wished for but couldn't find. The first Tabula is a Mac OS X program. In December 2009 the site offers beta .97 for Leopard and Snow Leopard, free, expiring on Christmas Day, and promises Version 1 available before Christmas! A phpBB forum opens in January 2010 with a thread asking what features people would like.

numeracyworks.com, October 2009 to January 2010 (Wayback Machine).

2010 – 2011

Rebuilt on Live Geometry

In 2010 Tabula is rewritten on the Live Geometry engine in Silverlight 4, so that it runs on Windows and on a Mac, in the browser or installed out of it. David becomes a co-developer of Live Geometry itself: the compass, ruler and protractor, the marker and eraser, the fold, knife and tape tools, a settings page and much else are written as extensions of the library, and the two projects share a codebase for a while. On May 2, 2011 Kirill announces Tabula on his blog with a four-minute video and ten screenshots, and asks readers to help David spread the news.

MSDN blog, May 2, 2011; the Live Geometry history, March to July 2010.

2011 – 2012

Tabula 2

The product site of 2011 and 2012 sells Tabula at $19.95 a copy through FastSpring, with classroom, lab, school and district licenses, and adds a web page export: any drawing becomes an interactive page played by a small Tabula Player. The site lists folding activities on YouTube, a page on paper folding in math education with a research bibliography, and quotes Arthur Lee of the University of Hong Kong and Jeff Hall of Mercer University. Wikipedia's article on interactive geometry software and the Silverlight Showcase list it.

numeracyworks.com, 2011 – 2012 (Wayback Machine).

2013 – 2015

Labs, and the end

By late 2013 the same shell hosts four apps: Tabula, a Patty Paper Lab, a Pattern Blocks Lab and a Pentagon Blocks Lab. Then Silverlight's own end comes; by August 2015 a reader of the 2011 blog post notes that the domain is vacant and that very little traces [are] left from Tabula. Nobody bought geometry, nobody could be reached who wanted it, and the platform it stood on went away. What remains is what the Internet Archive kept: this wing.

numeracyworks.com, August to December 2013 (Wayback Machine); comments on the MSDN blog, August 2015.

Tabula Details

Tabula was created by David Hartmann, a math teacher with 10 years of experience at both the high school and middle school levels. David created the program to help students learn and teachers teach abstract concepts through familiar manipulation of shapes. David also designed the program to make transformations as accessible to young minds as possible through animation. Teachers can use the program to lecture and present or to guide and extend activities. Students can use the program to learn and apply concepts. Hobbyists can use unique features such as folding to do what other programs can't. In educational settings Tabula is targeting 5th grade math through high school geometry classrooms.

Transformation

With the aid of animation and a pushpin, figures can be transformed in an intuitive and visually accessible way.

  • Rotate – define center and angle
  • Dilate – define center and scale factor
  • Reflect – define mirror figure
  • Translate – define magnitude & direction

Manipulation

Investigate geometric relationships and the results of subdividing and combining shapes. The manipulation tools are designed to mimic and extend what can be done with familiar materials and tools.

  • Fold figures such as segments, polygons and ellipticals leaving creases if desired.
  • Cut figures along a path.
  • Tape polygons together.
  • Use a pushpin when transforming.

Measurement

Use these instruments to measure and analyze figures. These instruments double as working models of real instruments. This is excellent for demonstrating usage. Models for both traditional and safety compasses are included.

  • Make construction arcs and circles.
  • Measure length in cm., in. or unitless.
  • Measure angles in radians and degrees.
  • Measure the area of polygons.
  • Study transformations, congruence, and symmetry with the mirror.

Presentation

With presentation technology such as an interactive whiteboard or projector and screen, Tabula can deliver interactive lessons and activities. Write, draw, and work with geometric objects in a new and exciting way.

  • Smartmarks remain attached to a figure.
  • Erase marks.
  • Add text and expressions.
  • Toggle the grid and axes.

More

  • Snapping – make relationships (midpoint, point on line, etc.) between figures by dragging them.
  • Style – change the appearance of figures.
  • File options – print or export your work as an image (png or bmp), web page, or as an xml.
  • Expressions – use constants, operations, functions, and references to figures in the workspace.

Construction

Tools for creating shapes are organized in trays. Some (not all) of the tools are listed below.

  • Basics – point, midpoint, segment, ray, line, polygon, circle, and more
  • Special Lines – parallel, perpendicular, horizontal, vertical, bisectors
  • Special Polygons – isosceles triangles, rectangles, trapezoids, and more
  • Transformations – rotated figure, reflected figure, translated figure, dilated figure, vector
  • Extras – half-plane, ellipse, bezier, locus, function, slider, and more
  • Custom – create and add your own tools (e.g. a parallelogram tool).

Paper Folding in Math Education

Paper Folding in Math Education

Throughout my career as a math teacher I've had students cut, fold, tape and manipulate paper in a number of ways. The goal was to learn geometric properties in an active, hands-on, and usually collaborative means. I always felt a great deal of success in this approach. Recently, I've been developing a computer program called Tabula that works with virtual shapes much as one works with paper shapes. My interest and knowledge in this kind of manipulation, in particular folding, has grown. I've created this web page to share what I've learned.

Benefits of Paper Folding

What are some of the benefits of paper folding? It is a manipulative. The effectiveness of manipulatives is well documented by research. It is accessible. Both ordinary paper and patty paper are cheap. It tends to be a positive experience. Coad makes a good case for the positive affect associated with paper folding. All traditional compass and straight-edge constructions can be performed plus additional constructions such as the trisection of an angle. There are also many resources available. Perhaps the best known resource in education is Michael Serra's Patty Paper Geometry.

Approaches To Paper Folding

One approach to paper folding is origami. Origami has the benefit of a long tradition that includes an artistic aspect. The research on paper folding is primarily on origami. Research by Norma Boakes, for example, suggests origami is an effective approach in the math classroom. Another approach to paper folding I've seen is a formalized/axiomatic one. Basic folds are established as axioms which sets up a system conducive to proofs and rigor. Olson and Coad provide good examples of this. One more approach, and these approaches are by no means exclusive, is an investigation approach. This is the approach I took. Folding is done to manifest properties and justification can be systematic or sometimes intuitive.

Dynamic Geometry Software and Paper Folding

What about dynamic geometry environment (DGE) and paper folding? I will shy away from saying anything definitive. Jones notes in his journal article that "DGE cannot provide a self-contained environment but that other activities are needed for students to make progress in mathematics." Perhaps paper folding could be one of the other activities. In the progression through the Van Hiele levels, paper folding as well as other manipulatives, would seem appropriate at an earlier stage than DGE. DGE is a great tool, but there are issues to consider such as the amount of procedural knowledge and student readiness. Perhaps for many, the best relationship for DGE and folding might be a complimentary one.

The first Tabula, for the Mac, 2009

Before the Silverlight rewrite the site read differently. The home page of October 2009: Tabula is a new software program for math instruction that joins the conveniences of a presentation program with the tools specific to teaching geometry. Tabula also excels at modeling math concepts such as fractions and factoring with geometry. A novel set of manipulation and transformation tools encourages activities that use paper, scissors, straight edge and other hands-on items. Tabula is tailored to late elementary and middle school math through high school geometry. It is best utilized on an electronic whiteboard (e.g. Smartboard®) or a computer connected to a projector or TV. Under Present · Transform · Manipulate · Measure and Construct: Fold, cut, tape, and use a pushpin just as they are used in the real world.

The download page of December 2009: Version 1 available before Christmas! Tabula is almost finished. I need teachers or indeed anyone to try the program and offer feedback. The free beta version now available for download still contains bugs yet is highly usable. No limitations are built in. It will expire on December 25th. Requirements: Mac OS X Leopard (10.5) or Snow Leopard (10.6). The current beta version is .97.

The details page of January 2010 lists what that version did: fold objects and create creases; cut objects along creases or anywhere else; tape objects back together; a pushpin that works just like it does on a pegboard; rotation, dilation, reflection and translation vectors placed in the workspace, with optional animation; a customizable grid with control over spacing, angles, thickness, and color. Includes isometric and 60º; a working demonstration compass, a ruler in centimeters and inches, a protractor; printing and export as TIFF and PDF; a boundless workspace.
